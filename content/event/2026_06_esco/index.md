abstract: "The use of Earth System Models in many-query applications, such as uncertainty<br>quantification and optimization, is limited by their high computational cost. Surrogates that<br>approximate the input-output maps are typically constructed and employed for computationally<br>intensive tasks. We will demonstrate methods to incorporate additional stochasticity into these<br>surrogates to characterize uncertainties arising from these models' intrinsic stochasticity. Such<br>generative, parametric-stochastic surrogates will then be employed in a model calibration loop in<br>which both physical parameters and parameters associated with structural model errors are<br>calibrated to fit observational data. With such embedded model-error statistical representations,<br>the calibrated model predictions remain consistent with the Earth System Model’s physical constraints, thanks to the non-intrusive nature of the model-error embedding. Besides, the<br>calibrated model is endowed with predictive uncertainty, which can be decomposed into<br>components such as intrinsic stochasticity, observational data noise, parametric uncertainty,<br>surrogate errors, and model structural errors. The methods will be demonstrated on various forms<br>of the Energy Exascale Earth System Model (E3SM), including the land component and the<br>analysis of the quasi-biennial oscillation."
